# Benchmark results — pure-Rust fp32 vs PyTorch fp32 ESMFold v1

15 diverse proteins (46–129 aa), folded with both implementations on the same
4-core / 15 GB CPU box. Per protein we recorded wall-clock time, peak RSS, pLDDT,
pTM, and the all-atom difference between the two predicted structures.

## Folder contents
- `metrics.csv` — full per-protein numbers (time, memory, pLDDT, pTM, RMSD, max dev).
- `figures/` — all PNG figures (dpi=300, bbox_inches=tight):
  - `fig1_structure_overlay.png` — 3D Cα backbone overlay (Rust vs PyTorch), 2 proteins.
  - `fig2_coord_scatter.png` — all backbone coords PyTorch-vs-Rust (identity) + deviation histogram.
  - `fig3_distance_maps.png` — Cα–Cα distance maps (PyTorch / Rust / |difference|), longest protein.
  - `fig4_accuracy_per_protein.png` — per-protein RMSD + max deviation (log scale).
  - `fig5_time_memory.png` — wall time and peak memory bar charts, both versions.

> The per-protein `pdb/` structures (`<name>_rust.pdb`, `<name>_pytorch.pdb`), the raw
> `dumps/` atom37 coordinates, and `proteins.fasta` are regenerated by the reproduce
> script below and are not checked into the repo.

## Key findings

**Accuracy.** 14 of 15 proteins agree to **0.00002–0.0023 Å** all-atom RMSD with pLDDT and
pTM identical to ≥3 decimals — i.e. the two predictions are the *same structure* to within
fp32 round-off. The single outlier, **sumo1 (0.14 Å)**, is expected, not a bug: SUMO1 has a
long disordered, low-confidence N-terminal tail, and on that flat energy landscape the tiny
~1e-6 fp32 differences in the trunk get amplified (chaotic sensitivity) into visible tail
motion. Its folded core still matches to mÅ and pLDDT/pTM stay ~equal (0.6440 vs 0.6437).
The mildly low-confidence proteins (bpti, insulin, pLDDT ~0.5–0.6) show the same effect at
~0.002 Å.

**Memory.** Rust uses **8.3–8.5 GB** vs PyTorch's flat **15.1 GB** (~45 % less). The Rust binary
memory-maps the weights and streams them; the fp32 PyTorch model only fits 15 GB at all when
run decomposed (LM, then folding head).

**Speed.** Rust is comparable on the smallest protein (crambin even slightly faster) and
~2–2.9× slower on larger ones. This is the expected hand-rolled-GEMM vs MKL gap and the
deliberate tradeoff for a dependency-free, single-process, fp32-exact binary. (PyTorch's
per-run time also includes loading its 11 GB model each time.)
